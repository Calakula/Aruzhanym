                Assignment #6. jQuery (Selectors, Effects, Animations)

Objective:
By the end of this assignment, students will be able to:
  • find any element on a page with jQuery selectors and traversal methods;
  • add, move and remove elements and show the changes with jQuery effects;
  • make a page come alive with fades, flights and sliding cards.

Task 1. Selector Detective (selectors and traversal)
AITU students met at a birthday party in a house. The guests were in 4 rooms from 19:30 to 22:30.
At 21:30 the birthday cake disappeared from the kitchen. The picture shows everyone who was there:
where they stood, what they wore, when they came and what they had with them. Rebuild the party
on a web page and find the thief with jQuery.
  1. Write the party from the picture as an array: rooms with their name and floor, and guests from
      left to right with the name, clothing color, arrival time, AITU student or not, and things.
  2. Build the page from the array with jQuery: 2 floors with rooms, and a card for every guest with
      the avatar, a ring in the clothing color, name, time, icons of the things and an AITU badge.
      Store the guest data on the card as classes and data-* attributes. The design is yours.
  3. Add a button for every case. A click highlights what the case finds, makes the found guests
      jump once with animate() and shows their number and names.
  4. Solve every case with one jQuery chain that searches the page, not the array. Before every case,
      build the page again; cases 18 and 19 change the party with fadeIn() and fadeOut().
  5. In your report, show every case: its jQuery chain and a screenshot of the result on the page.

Task 2. The party in motion (effects and animations)
Create a copy of your Task 1 files and continue in the copy. Make the page come alive with effects.
   1. Spotlight. Click a case. The guests that the case finds stay bright. All other guests become
       pale: they fade to 20% in 0.3 seconds. Some cases find rooms, not guests. For these cases,
       the guests in all the other rooms become pale. The Clear button makes all guests bright
       again.
   2. Guest card. Click a guest. A card with all the information about this guest opens. The card
       shows the information in words, for example “Came at 20:30”, “AITU student”, “Has a
       phone and a bag”. The card does not just appear: it starts small on the guest’s photo and
       grows to its full size. The card closes when the user presses Esc or clicks outside the card.
       Then it gets small again and goes back into the photo. Only one card can be open. When the
       user clicks another guest, the first card closes and the new one opens.
   3. Move. The guest card has a button for each other room, for example “Move to the hall”.
       Click a button to send the guest to the end of that room. The guest’s card flies across the
       house to the new room; it does not jump. In the old room, the empty place closes slowly. In
       the new room, a place opens slowly. After the move, change your array too, so that the cases
       from Task 1 find the guest in the new room.
   4. Sort by time. Each room has a button “Sort by time”. Click it, and the guests of this room
       stand in order of their arrival time, from the earliest to the latest. Click it again, and the
       guests go back to the order in the picture. The cards move slowly to their new places; they
       do not jump.
   5. Fast clicks. Click the buttons and guests very fast, many times. Every effect must still end
       in the right state. No animation repeats again and again after the user stops clicking.
